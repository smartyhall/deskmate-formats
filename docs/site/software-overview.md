# Software and releases

DeskMate was not a single package that remained unchanged. Its desktop, bundled applications, hardware requirements, and third-party ecosystem developed over a series of releases. This part of the reference is a guide to the software itself: what was offered, what has survived, what we have installed, and how the different versions compare.

If you are new to DeskMate, begin with [Discover DeskMate](../history/introduction.md). If you already know the version you used, start with the installation index or application inventory below.

## A family of different desktops

The [DeskMate Evolution Atlas](../ecosystem/deskmate-evolution-atlas.md) brings together the original text-oriented environment, Personal DeskMate, later graphical releases, and machine-specific OEM editions. It pairs the release evidence with native screen captures where available. This is the best place to see how the software changed over time, rather than assume that one desktop represents every edition.

The [installation media index](../ecosystem/deskmate-installations.md) is the practical companion to the Atlas. It records the distributions we have identified, the media they use, and the evidence behind our installation observations. Where a release has only been identified or tested on related hardware, the records distinguish that from a complete native installation.

For a closer look at two late releases, follow the [DeskMate 3.03.01 installation guide](../ecosystem/deskmate-installation-303.md), the [DeskMate 3.05 installation guide](../ecosystem/deskmate-installation-305.md), and the [native comparison of 3.03.01 and 3.05](../research/deskmate-303-305-comparison.md). These document specific tested environments, not instructions guaranteed to work on every PC.

## What could you do with DeskMate?

The [illustrated application inventory](../inventory.md) introduces the Desktop, its common controls, and the principal programs. It also points to what we have established about their files. Read it if you remember a particular program but do not know its file extension or exact version.

Two examples show why looking beyond the standard application names matters. [Note Pad, Corkboard, and Information Center](../history/note-pad-corkboard.md) follows the small note-taking accessory into later desktop and household interfaces. [Automatic House, Plug 'n Power, and the CP290](../research/deskmate-automatic-house-x10.md) documents the home-automation software and measured controller interactions, distinguishing the simulated serial experiment from physical hardware operation.

## Beyond the bundled software

DeskMate also supported a wider collection of commercial programs, companion modules, utilities, and applications shipped with their own runtime. Our [ecosystem catalog](../ecosystem/README.md) identifies those products and explains how the collection is being documented. Its machine-readable catalog distinguishes historical identification, archived media, installation, and file-format research.

The [TVDog descriptive directory index review](../ecosystem/tvdog-directory-index-review.md) explains how the original archive descriptions helped us identify programs that would have been difficult to recognize by filename alone. The [acquisition gaps](../ecosystem/acquisition-gaps.md) identify preserved candidates that need further qualification and products for which verified media is still missing. A listing or a recovered disk is not proof that a program has been successfully installed or fully understood.
