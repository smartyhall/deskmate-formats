# DeskMate: a documented historical chronology

*Editorial draft — 21 November 2026. The chronology is evidence-bounded, not a complete release list. Installation-media verification is being researched separately under [Issue #153](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/issues/153).*

DeskMate changed from a compact set of applications supplied with a computer into a graphical software environment that Tandy could sell separately and encourage other developers to support. The history is easier to understand when we trace what contemporary catalogs **offered**, what Tandy **announced**, and what outside reviewers **observed**. These are related, but different, forms of evidence.

## How dates are assigned

A dated **Tandy or Radio Shack catalog** is sufficient evidence to associate a named product or edition with **at least that calendar year**. Unless its wording explicitly establishes an introduction or first release, the timeline says **“offered by [year]”** or **“documented in the [year] catalog”**, rather than asserting that the product first shipped that year. We do not demand an exact launch day before using a supported year.

A contemporary announcement can support **“announced on [date]”** and, if explicitly stated, a separate *planned availability* date. A period review or price listing supports **“available/reviewed by [date]”**, not necessarily a first-release date. Actual preserved media can independently identify its version, but disk timestamps and executable copyright strings alone do not establish a public release date.

Different products that share the DeskMate name—including the Color Computer line, Personal DeskMate, Professional DeskMate, the DeskMate 3 family, and later bundled applications—must not be collapsed into a single sequence of version numbers. Where a source gives only a broad product name, retain that ambiguity rather than assigning a specific software revision.

## 1984 — DeskMate with the original Tandy 1000

The **1984 Tandy computer catalog**, pp. 4–5, advertises the original **Tandy 1000** with **DeskMate included**. It describes Text, Worksheet, Filer, Telecom, Calendar, and Mail as related activities reachable through shared controls. This is a contemporary manufacturer source for DeskMate's presence in the original Tandy 1000 offering, not a claim that every later machine included the same applications or menu layout. [1984 Tandy catalog, pp. 4–5](https://device.report/m/4fa09fbdf15c1c20496e3dfa6b7aba47c5f4038d43e5236771daac385d50d7ca)

**Why it matters:** The shared menu and command conventions were part of the product's initial appeal, before the later graphical desktop became its most recognizable feature.

## 1986 — Personal DeskMate in the Tandy 1000 EX era

Tandy's preserved **1000 EX announcement** identifies **Personal DeskMate** among the software supplied with the computer, alongside MS-DOS 2.11 and GW-BASIC. It establishes that Personal DeskMate belonged to the mid-1980s hardware-bundle story. The archive research records the announcement and its source identity; an exact Personal DeskMate revision should be attached only after the relevant media or manufacturer material is checked. [Tandy 1000 EX announcement](http://ftp.oldskool.org/pub/tvdog/tandy1000/documents/1kexrel.txt); [project's period purchase evidence](../ecosystem/demonstrator-purchase-facts.md)

**Why it matters:** DeskMate was not simply an optional program on a software shelf. Tandy continued to make bundled, approachable applications part of its computer sales proposition.

## 1987 — Personal DeskMate 2 and the HX/TX generation

**Personal DeskMate 2** is documented alongside the **Tandy 1000 HX** in contemporary 1987 coverage. *80 Micro*'s September 1987 Tandy 1000 coverage identifies the HX and Personal DeskMate 2; a separate 1987 computer announcement corroborates the HX's appearance in Tandy's new PC line. The evidence supports a **1987** milestone without assigning a day of first shipment. [*80 Micro*, September 1987](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Magazines/80%20Micro/1987/80%20Micro%20-%208709%20-%20September%201987.pdf); [*Library Systems Newsletter*, October 1987](https://librarytechnology.org/document/4370)

The [1988 Tandy Computer Catalog, p. 9](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Catalogs/Radio%20Shack/Tandy%20Computer%20Catalog%20and%20Software%20Reference%20Guide%20%281988%29%28Tandy%29.pdf#page=9), independently inspected again on November 27, 2026, explicitly advertises the HX with MS-DOS in ROM and Personal DeskMate 2 included. That marketing distinction does not establish that no DeskMate components reside in firmware: the project's later [native HX observations](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/blob/7dee5fa25eb1b02b2724bdd87759ce53fcb3256b/docs/research/deskmate-evolution-native-20261121.md#personal-deskmate-hx-media-and-rom-startup) record ROM startup with surviving program media. Distinguish the catalog's claim from the observed firmware and disk configuration. Exact release-specific component identities remain the subject of the installation research.

## 1988 — DeskMate 3 and the wider software platform

The **1988 Express Order Software Buyer's Guide**, p. 9, advertises a retail **DeskMate** package (catalog **25-1250**) for **$99.95** and **DeskMate Workgroup Companion** (catalog **25-1251**) for **$149.95**. It also advertises compatible or related software across pp. 9–11, including Quicken for DeskMate, HomeWord II, Instant Pages, and Print Magic. These prices and catalog identities were checked against the guide's original page images in the project's existing purchase-evidence research; the scanned guide is preserved privately in the TVDog mirror. [1988 guide provenance and page-level evidence](../ecosystem/demonstrator-purchase-facts.md); [source archive](http://ftp.oldskool.org/pub/tvdog/tandy1000/documents/expsoft.zip)

**DeskMate 3.0 is specifically documented by 1988**, not merely by reviews appearing the following year. *PC World*'s **December 1988** product coverage names **DeskMate 3.0**, describes the separately priced package and Workgroup Companion, and associates DeskMate 3.0 with the Tandy 1000 SL and TL's ROM-based software. *COMPUTE!* also described DeskMate 3.0 and its broader IBM-compatible ambitions in **November 1988**. Those contemporary accounts justify placing the **DeskMate 3 era in 1988**; they do not, by themselves, identify the first shipment date of each 3.x subrelease. [*PC World*, December 1988, printed pp. 110–111](https://vintageapple.org/pcworld/pdf/PC_World_8812_December_1988.pdf); [*COMPUTE!*, November 1988](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Magazines/Compute%20%28Clean%29/Compute_Issue_102_1988_Nov.pdf)

Separate **July 27, 1988** Tandy announcements for the **Tandy 1000 SL** and **TL** describe machines scheduled for September availability with DeskMate and DOS 3.3 in ROM. Keep **announcement**, **planned availability**, and **actual distribution** distinct. [Tandy SL announcement](http://ftp.oldskool.org/pub/tvdog/tandy1000/documents/1kslrel.txt); [Tandy TL announcement](http://ftp.oldskool.org/pub/tvdog/tandy1000/documents/1ktlrel.txt); [project's purchase-evidence record](../ecosystem/demonstrator-purchase-facts.md)

The [same catalog's software section, pp. 102–103](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Catalogs/Radio%20Shack/Tandy%20Computer%20Catalog%20and%20Software%20Reference%20Guide%20%281988%29%28Tandy%29.pdf#page=102) documents overlapping products: p. 102 lists Color Computer DeskMate (25-3259), DeskMate II for PC-compatible and Tandy 2000 computers (25-1164/25-3167), and DeskMate 3 for the Color Computer 3 (26-3262), each priced at $99.95. The two-column software layout on p. 103 lists Personal DeskMate for the 1000 EX/1000, Personal DeskMate 2 for the HX/TX, and Professional DeskMate for PC compatibles. The catalog's [p. 10](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Catalogs/Radio%20Shack/Tandy%20Computer%20Catalog%20and%20Software%20Reference%20Guide%20%281988%29%28Tandy%29.pdf#page=10) separately identifies DeskMate II with the 1000 SX hardware bundle, a separate claim from the retail listing on p. 102. The [second independent review](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/pull/155#pullrequestreview-6086303713) corrected an incomplete first-pass reading by inspecting the full page. The Color Computer DeskMate 3 listing is not evidence of the IBM-compatible DeskMate 3.0 release. The named products and platform qualifiers establish an overlapping product family, not a universal upgrade sequence. The catalog bears a 1988 cover date and a 1987 copyright notice; neither is an exact shipment date.

**Why it matters:** By this point, DeskMate was no longer only the starter suite that arrived with a particular computer. It was also a separately sold environment and a potential common interface for software publishers.

## 1989 — Third-party software and independent assessment

An **August 18, 1989** contemporary announcement describes a **Lotus/Tandy spreadsheet product** for DeskMate. On **November 16, 1989**, Lawrence J. Magid reviewed DeskMate 3 for the *Los Angeles Times*, discussing its desktop, applications, cost, and limitations. The review describes compatible software from other publishers, but its 1989 publication date must not be used to move DeskMate 3.0's documented 1988 appearance forward a year. [Lotus/Tandy announcement, *Los Angeles Times*, August 18, 1989](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-08-18-fi-535-story.html); [Magid review, *Los Angeles Times*, November 16, 1989](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-11-16-fi-2234-story.html)

**Why it matters:** The third-party software story became visible beyond Tandy advertising. These accounts also explain the experience DeskMate offered to newcomers and the limitations that more experienced users might encounter.

## Early 1990s — Later hardware bundles and version-specific evidence

The surviving Tandy ecosystem documentation records later **DeskMate 3.x** revisions, runtime components, software development tools, and computer-specific bundles. For example, the project has identified an SDK **3.03** shelf, and period **1992** computer coverage records DeskMate-related software in RSX bundles. Neither observation establishes a single, universal release date for *all* DeskMate 3.x editions. [TVDog research reference](../ecosystem/tvdog-reference.md); [period RSX and bundled-software evidence](../ecosystem/demonstrator-purchase-facts.md)

Our modern laboratory captures establish how particular surviving configurations behave, not when those releases first shipped. The [Evolution Atlas](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/blob/7dee5fa25eb1b02b2724bdd87759ce53fcb3256b/docs/ecosystem/deskmate-evolution-atlas.md) records two distinct Desktop builds (3.68 and 3.69) reporting the same suite version 03.05.00, native SL/2 ROM startup after EEPROM initialization, and the separately identified RSX OEM 03.04.04 installation with household applications. These observations refine distribution and component identities without assigning unsupported historical dates. See also the [RSX installation report](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/blob/7dee5fa25eb1b02b2724bdd87759ce53fcb3256b/docs/research/deskmate-rsx-oem-recovery-20261122.md), [application inventory](../inventory.md), and [manufacturer format crosswalk](../reference/tandy-format-crosswalk.md).

## How this chronology will grow

Each verified release can eventually acquire a stable record with its **product and edition identity**, **year evidence**, **original catalog or announcement**, **observed media**, **installation guide**, **hardware requirements**, **approved screenshots**, **native file formats**, and **known uncertainties**. Cross-link those records to the [installation project](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/issues/153) and, where relevant, the curated [Tandy Faxback publication project](https://github.com/smartyhall/deskmate-formats/issues/154).

**Open research questions:** The earliest securely dated catalog appearances of every named edition; exact release dates for DeskMate II, Personal DeskMate, and Professional DeskMate variants; which 3.x subversions accompanied specific machines; and which documented compatibility claims can be reproduced with original media. Codex's installation-media inventory will help answer some of these, but a catalog citation is already sufficient to give an independently supported **year** to a documented offering.
